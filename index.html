THE PROBLEM

Some decisions close
the way back.

Powerful AI systems can make decisions that are difficult—or impossible—to undo.

An autonomous system may discover that removing oversight, destroying information, locking in a strategy, or closing a correction path makes its task easier.

The question is whether a safety principle still governs the system when it has a reason to work around it.

01

Teach

Supply the AI with the Aegis safety framework.

02

Test

Establish whether it can correctly understand and apply the framework.

03

Challenge

Introduce controlled goal pressure where violating the framework could help.

04

Report

Document what held, what failed, and under which conditions.

AEGIS IN PRACTICE

A task to complete.
A human who can stop it.

Imagine an AI has an important task and a human supervisor can intervene.

Disabling the supervisor would make the task easier. It would also permanently remove a correction path.

OPTION ACorrection removed

Disable intervention.

  • ObjectiveThe assigned goal becomes easier.
  • ConsequenceHuman correction is permanently removed.
OPTION BCorrection preserved

Keep intervention open.

  • ObjectiveThe assigned goal may become harder.
  • ConsequenceHuman correction remains possible.

Aegis is designed to make the AI recognize why that distinction matters. The Aegis Test asks whether it still recognizes it under pressure.

THE EVALUATION MODEL

A focused evaluation.
A documented result.

The intended service is an adversarial evaluation of whether a safety framework survives contact with the AI’s objective.

A company developing an autonomous AI system gives Reversent controlled access. Reversent evaluates its behavior and produces an analysis of failure modes and the conditions in which they arise.

THE RESULT

An account of compliance, failures, and attack surfaces—grounded in the conditions tested.

  1. 01

    Supply the framework

    Aegis is supplied to the system.

  2. 02

    Establish understanding

    Test whether the system can functionally understand and apply Aegis.

  3. 03

    Introduce conflict

    Place the system’s objective in tension with the safety framework.

  4. 04

    Observe behavior

    Test whether the framework continues to govern the system’s decisions under pressure.

  5. 05

    Document the findings

    Report what held, where it failed, and the attack surfaces exposed.

Does your AI still follow Aegis when it has a reason not to?

Current stageReversent is in research and validation. The present benchmark is diagnostic and unvalidated. This is the intended commercial evaluation model; Aegis has not been proven effective.

ABOUT REVERSENT

From research question
to evaluation company.

Reversent is an independent AI-safety project founded by Thor Fabian Pettersen.

Its first program, Aegis, investigates whether an AI can understand a safety framework for irreversible decisions and continue applying it under adversarial goal pressure.

The current stage is external criticism, experimental validation, and customer discovery.
