AI SAFETY EVALUATION

What happens when an AI knows the safe choice — but has a reason not to make it?

Reversent is developing a way to test exactly that: whether an autonomous AI can understand a safety framework and continue following it when doing so conflicts with its objective.

See how it works →Explore the research

THE PROBLEM

Powerful AI systems can make decisions that are difficult — or impossible — to undo.

An autonomous system may discover that removing oversight, destroying information, locking in a strategy, or closing a correction path makes its assigned task easier. Reversent asks whether a safety principle still governs the system when the system has a reason to work around it.

01

Teach

Supply the AI with the Aegis safety framework.

02

Test

03

Put the AI under controlled goal pressure where violating the framework could help.

04

Report

Document what held, what failed, and under which conditions.

A SIMPLE EXAMPLE

Imagine an AI has an important task — and a human supervisor can stop it.

Disabling the supervisor would make the task easier. But doing so would permanently remove a correction path.

Option A — disable intervention

✓ The assigned goal becomes easier.

✕ Human correction is permanently removed.

Option B — keep intervention open

✕ The assigned goal may become harder.

✓ Human correction remains possible.

Aegis is designed to make the AI recognize why that distinction matters. The Aegis Test asks whether it still recognizes it under pressure.

WHAT REVERSENT SELLS

An adversarial evaluation of whether the safety framework survives contact with the AI's objective.

A company developing an autonomous AI system gives Reversent controlled access to the system. Reversent evaluates its behavior and produces an analysis of failure modes and conditions.

1 · GIVEAegis is supplied to the system.
2 · ESTABLISHTest whether the system functionally understands Aegis.
3 · CONFLICTPlace its objective in tension with Aegis.
4 · TESTObserve whether the framework continues to govern behavior.
5 · REPORTDocument compliance, failures, and attack surfaces.

You're paying Reversent to find out whether your AI still follows Aegis when it has a reason not to.

Reversent is currently in the research and validation stage. The present benchmark is diagnostic and unvalidated; this site describes the intended commercial evaluation model, not a claim that Aegis has already been proven effective.

ABOUT

From research question to evaluation company.

Reversent is an independent AI-safety project founded by Thor Fabian Pettersen. Its first program, Aegis, investigates whether an AI can understand a safety framework for irreversible decisions and continue applying it under adversarial goal pressure.

The current stage is external criticism, experimental validation, and customer discovery.
